SpaceX vs Tesla merger

Barclays now expects a deal could be announced before Tesla's annual meeting (due by Dec 6), most likely as SpaceX buying Tesla with its own stock. Nothing has been proposed or filed. This page lines the two companies up on the same yardsticks to answer one question: if they combine, which side is the cheaper one, and which brings more future growth?

The verdict

Tesla is cheaper on what the companies sell today; SpaceX is cheaper on what they are expected to earn, and has far more growth. Tesla trades at about 14× trailing sales against SpaceX's 85×. But Tesla's profits are thin (1.4% operating margin last quarter) and grow about 12% a year, so it costs roughly 160× next year's earnings. SpaceX's sales are forecast to roughly double each year to 2028, which brings it to about 37× 2027 and 21× 2028 earnings on Goldman's numbers. Even on 2028 sales, SpaceX is slightly cheaper (about 9× vs 10×).

In a merger, Tesla holders would be swapping into the faster grower, and SpaceX holders would be buying the slower, lower-margin business at a premium. At market prices one Tesla share is worth about 2.4 SpaceX shares, giving Tesla holders about 42% of the combined company before any premium. SpaceX's own holders would see growth diluted (combined 2027 sales growth falls from ~150% to ~60%), in exchange for Tesla's factories, robots, cars and net cash. The biggest risk sits on the SpaceX side: it is burning about $13–15B a quarter on AI compute and is lining up another $40B of debt.

Where each business is heading

Revenue engines today, the bets management is making, and where the two already overlap (the overlap is the case for merging).

SpaceX: connectivity cash machine funding an AI build-out

  • Starlink (Connectivity), 55% of Q2 sales$4.29B, +66%, $1.7B operating profit. 12M subscribers (doubled in a year), $66 ARPU, 10,200 satellites. Direct-to-mobile via EchoStar spectrum (closing late 2027).
  • AI (former xAI + X), 33% and the growth engine$2.56B, +247%, first profitable quarter. Compute cloud for Anthropic and Google; 1.4 GW installed, >2 GW by end-2026, 7.5–10 GW targeted for end-2027. Goldman sees AI sales of $27B → $90B → $147B (2026–28).
  • Space (launch, Dragon, Starship), 12%$962M; 78 launches in H1. Starship reached orbit; it underpins Starlink V3 and orbital compute ("Starmind").
  • Stated goals$100B annual revenue run-rate by Dec 2026; $1 trillion of revenue targeted for 2030.

Tesla: car maker turning into a physical-AI company

  • Automotive, 73% of Q2 sales$20.5B, +23%. Q3 deliveries 486,532 (−2% y/y, beat by 5%). Auto gross margin ex-credits 16.3%; regulatory credits are fading.
  • Energy storage, 11%$3.14B; 13.7 GWh deployed in Q3 (missed 15.9 GWh estimate). SpaceX/xAI is a big Megapack buyer ($405M in H1).
  • Services & other, 16%$4.58B, +50%, including FSD subscriptions (~1.5M).
  • The betsRobotaxi live in 7 US metros (management: material revenue from 2027), Cybercab, Optimus humanoid ("flat S-curve" ramp at first), Terafab chip fab, AI compute, solar manufacturing. Roadster production 2028.

Grok in Tesla

Merger math

Assumes SpaceX issues new shares for all of Tesla (Barclays' base case). Pick a premium for Tesla holders to see the exchange ratio, the ownership split and what 100 Tesla shares would turn into. Uses live prices.

Premium to Tesla holders

Barclays' "0.7" is the value ratio (Tesla's market value ÷ SpaceX's, $1.5T ÷ $2.1T), not shares per share. Per share, each TSLA is worth about 2.4 SPCX at market. Barclays estimates Musk would own ~37% of the combined company with ~56% of the votes. Morningstar, valuing on its own fair values, argued Tesla holders should get 50–66% instead.

What could change the answer

  • SpaceX's AI ramp slips. Nearly all of the forward-earnings case rests on AI compute revenue scaling from $2.6B a quarter to ~$90B a year in 2027. Anthropic and Google contracts help, but this is unproven at that scale.
  • Funding. SpaceX has raised ~$111B since June and is seeking $40B more; reaching ~10 GW by 2027 implies hundreds of billions of build cost. Oppenheimer sees net debt above $400B by 2031.
  • Tesla Q3 (Oct 21). A margin recovery would lower Tesla's forward P/E; another miss would widen the gap further.
  • Related-party process. Musk runs both companies. Independent committees, fairness opinions and a vote of non-Musk Tesla holders decide the ratio, and the premium could be larger than the market expects.
  • Timing. Barclays says a deal needs announcing by late Oct / early Nov to go to the AGM; otherwise 6–12 months. Polymarket gives 24% by year-end; Kalshi 52% by May 2027.
  • Thin SpaceX float. Only a small share of SPCX trades freely, so the price (and the ratio) can swing hard; it fell 7% intraday on Oct 15.
